Door 03

An ad got rejected

Assume a bot did it. Pull the creative, change one small thing, republish it once. Do not request review. Here is the full protocol, plus the exact words that get our ads flagged.

01

First, look before you act

A rejected ad is not a restricted account. Those are two different things, and mixing them up is how people panic themselves into bad decisions.

Open Account Quality and read what it actually says. Then open Ads Manager and filter by delivery status so you are looking at today's status, not the status you remember from last week.

Here is why that matters. In July 2026 our account took eight rejections in a row, every one under the same policy: "Financial and insurance products and services." It felt like the account was in trouble. It was not. The ad account stayed in good standing the whole time, no spend cap, no penalty. And by mid-August every one of those rejections had cleared on its own, with no notice from Meta. For weeks we kept calling those ads "blocked" from memory while they were quietly live.

So: check live, every time. Meta clears rejections silently.

The other thing to hold onto is that rejections right now are mostly automated. Keala calls it a pendulum. For a stretch, Meta lets almost anything through. Then it swings the other way and the bots start flagging things that were fine a month ago. If your ad got rejected this week, the most likely reviewer was a system, not a person. That shapes everything that follows.

02

The protocol

This is our rule. It replaces "request review" everywhere you see it, including in older team docs.

Step 01
Delete the creative, not the ad

Open the rejected ad and remove the media that got flagged: the video or the image. Leave the ad itself in place. You are not deleting the whole ad yet, and you are not pausing it either, because pausing leaves it sitting in the review queue. The ad shell stays, the flagged file goes.

Step 02
Change one small thing

For a video: trim a second off, swap the background music, add one or two seconds at the front or back, move a cut, or change one of the images. For a static: change one word, or just open it and re-save it. The point is a new file. A new file carries new metadata, and to Meta it reads as new creative.

One big exception: if the reason says "Prohibited commercial practices," skip this step. A small edit changes the file, not the message, so it gets rejected again. Go to that section instead.

Step 03
Upload the edited file into that same ad and publish

Drop the new file into the ad you just emptied and hit publish. Because the file is new, Meta reviews it as new creative, and you have not touched the campaign settings around it, which is what would send other ads back through review.

Step 04
Rejected again? Now delete the whole ad and move on

One re-upload. That is the whole budget. This is the point where the entire ad comes out of the account, so it stops showing in Account Quality. If it comes back rejected a second time, something in the creative itself is tripping the classifier, and guessing at it costs you approvals you cannot get back.

We learned this the expensive way. July 2026, one ad set, six guess-and-resubmit rejections. Meta's rejection notice names no phrase and gives no signal, so we gained exactly zero information and burned six strikes against our ratio.

Step 05
Fix the language before anything else goes up

Take the rejected concept back to the script. Run every line against the flagged-words box below. Then write the next version clean, and only then film or design it.

03

If the reason says "Prohibited commercial practices"

This one is different. Meta read your ad as a job offer or a promise about pay. It is not a bot glitch, so the one-retry rule above does not apply.

Trimming the video or changing the music gives Meta a new file with the same words in it, and the same words get the same rejection. Every retry is one more strike against your ratio.

Here is how fast it stacks. One of our members had 12 ads rejected for this reason, then Meta restricted her entire account because her ads were "repeatedly" breaking the rules. Her hooks were about a great paycheck, working 50 weeks a year, and the job not being the problem. She never had a single rejection she thought was serious, until all of them together were.

Stop the bleeding, today

  1. Check Account Quality first. If anything is already listed as restricted, you are past this section. Go to Door 04.
  2. Delete the rejected ads. No retry, no small edit, no review request.
  3. Turn off any live ad that uses the same kind of language, even if it has not been flagged yet. Those are your next rejections. Turning an ad off is safe. Editing a live ad is what sends it back through review.
  4. Create a spare ad account now, while you still can. Once an ad account is restricted, you can never create another one in that Business Manager. Door 05 shows how.
  5. Start the engagement layer. One separate campaign, 3 to 4 plain value posts, a dollar a day. It rebuilds your approval ratio while you rewrite. Door 02 has the settings.

Rewrite from scratch, do not edit

Anything that sounds like hiring or earning comes out: job, paycheck, salary, position, apply, hiring, "looking for people," "join my team," hours or weeks of work, replacing an income, any dollar figure. Those words are now in the flagged-words box.

Then tell your own story. Describe the feeling and the life you have now, never how the money works or how someone could join you.

Then go back on offense

  1. Send your rewritten ads to Nat before they run.
  2. Launch them in a new campaign, not an old one.
  3. Three ads at a time. Wait for them to clear, then add the next three. If one gets flagged, you find out on three ads, not twelve.
04

Do this, not that

DoDo not
Pull the flagged creative out of the ad the day it happens, edit it, and republish it once. Request review. It puts the ad in front of a human, and a human can decide to look at the whole account. Keala on a mentee who appealed: "I'm pretty sure that's why Natasha's now banned."
Make one small edit and upload once. Pause it and leave it sitting in the queue.
Check Account Quality and live ad status before you decide anything. Call an ad "blocked" from memory. Meta clears rejections without telling you.
Keep your engagement campaign running through all of this. It is the thing rebuilding your ratio. Resubmit variant three, four, and five hoping one slips through.
If videos keep getting blocked after the one re-upload, move the concept to image ads and put the full video on the landing page instead. Images clear more easily and CPMs come down. Re-test video later. Touch the live campaign the rejected ad came from. Budget, targeting, anything.
Read the rejection reason once, then stop trying to reverse-engineer it. Self-declare into a Special Ad Category to "get it through." That reclassifies you and strips targeting from everything.
The one exception

If your whole account gets reclassified into a Special Ad Category (credit, employment, housing, or insurance), that is worth a review request, because it damages every campaign you run and it will not clear on its own. A single ad carrying a special-category label is a different thing: just delete it. And never self-declare into a Special Ad Category.

05

The Canary Test

After you have deleted the rejected ads, run one plain photo as an engagement ad. Nothing to do with the business. A picture of your dog, your coffee, the view from your window.

If that gets approved, the problem was the creative. Fix the language and carry on.

If that gets rejected, the problem is not your ad. Something on your page or your account has been flagged, and no amount of editing a video will fix it. That is a Door 04 situation. Head there.

06

If the whole account picked up a financial-category flag

Sometimes the classifier stops flagging ads and starts categorizing the account itself as a financial advertiser. You will see it as the same policy hitting everything, including creative that has nothing to do with money.

Keala's read: this is close to impossible to remove. The realistic routes are an agency ad account (gated behind big spend), or months of support tickets hoping to reach a person. Neither is a plan.

The move is to start a fresh ad account and season it with engagement ads first, while you leave whatever is still running on the old account alone. New ad accounts are normal. Sometimes they are better, because you are starting with a clean learning history instead of one that has been trained on the wrong signals.

You will want a spare ad account already sitting there for this. If you do not have one, Door 05 shows you how to create it.

07

Words that get us flagged

The classifier does not read meaning. It reads tokens. A sentence that is clearly about frustration will still get flagged if it contains the word "income" next to the word "ceiling." So we treat the list below as hard law on anything that appears in an ad: the image, the caption, the headline, the first frames of the video.

The rule of thumb: describe the feeling and the life, not the formula. Time, energy, Sundays, dread, choice, room to breathe, a skill she learns. Never how the money works.

Ceiling language
income ceiling no ceiling capped uncapped
The pay-model frame
the way you get paid a different pay model the math It's not you. It's the math.
Replacement and freedom
replace your income quit your job income financial freedom
Numbers
Any dollar figure. Any income screenshot. Any earnings claim, stated or implied.
Promises
passive income residual income money while you sleep guaranteed proven to earn results in X days
Job-offer language
job paycheck salary position apply hiring looking for people join my team weeks of work (these trigger "Prohibited commercial practices")
Category tokens
MLM pyramid recruiting scam fraud union (that last one is a Personal Attributes token)
Personal attributes
Are you a mom who...? and any stated age. Describe, do not accuse: "To the mama who..." instead. Never put a number on her age.
One place the mentors disagree

Keala's view (Sep 2026) is that naming an income ceiling as a pain is not the same as promising an income, and that it is worth testing in a static image. Our own account history says that exact phrase got flagged, eight times. So the site position is: high-risk. Test it only if you are willing to take the hit to your ratio, and only in an image, never in a video hook.

The income ladder

Safest is not to mention income at all. Safer is third-party framing ("the simple system behind women who earn..."), which is still not clean. Riskiest is direct second-person how-to ("build a $5k month"). Meta also judges mostly what is seen right after the click, so a line buried deep in the webclass is far lower risk than the same line on the landing page.

And for statics specifically, the shortest version of all of this: no claims in images, no flags.

08

After it clears

Go back to the habits in Door 01 and keep the volume going in Door 02. One rejection inside fifty clean approvals barely registers. One rejection inside twelve is a trend.

And before you tell anyone an ad is still rejected, check it live one more time. It probably is not.
