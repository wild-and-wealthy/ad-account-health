How to keep Meta ad accounts alive, what to do when an ad gets rejected, and what to do when something bigger breaks.

02

The 60-second version

If you read nothing else, read these eight points. They hold no matter which door you are standing in.

  1. Run ads from a Business Manager ad account. Never from your personal profile's business manager, and never from the Instagram boost button. A restriction there can take your whole profile and your Instagram down with it.
  2. Read Account Quality before you touch anything, and check an ad's status live in Ads Manager before you call it blocked. Meta clears rejections silently. Every one of ours did.
  3. A rejected ad is a bot decision. Pull the flagged creative out of the ad, make one small edit to it, put it back and republish. Do not request review. If it gets rejected a second time, delete the whole ad and move on. One exception: if the reason is "Prohibited commercial practices," the words have to change, not the file. Here's what to do.
  4. Delete rejected ads. Toggle off losing ads. Never the other way around. The account's learning lives in your losers; rejections live in your ratio.
  5. The account is scored on approvals versus rejections over roughly the last 30 days. A separate, always-on engagement campaign with 3 to 4 ads in an ad set at even just a dollar a day is how we keep that ratio healthy.
  6. Do not touch a live campaign during a crackdown. Changing budget or targeting, or switching an old campaign back on, can push it back through review. New work goes in a new campaign.
  7. Two policies catch almost everyone: Personal Attributes and Financial Claims. No claims in images. Describe the feeling and the life, not the formula. Our flagged-word list is the checklist.
  8. Verify the business and create a spare ad account now, while everything is healthy. Both take minutes. Both are painful to need and not have.
The one exception to "never request review"

If your whole account gets reclassified into a Special Ad Category (credit, employment, housing, insurance), request review, because that hits every campaign. A single ad with a special-category label gets deleted. And never self-declare into a Special Ad Category to get an ad through.

03

Check your standing right now

Five minutes. Do this before you pick a door, and again every week.

  • Open facebook.com/accountquality. Is any restriction listed on your profile, business, ad account, or page? Are any ads listed as rejected?
  • In Ads Manager, filter by delivery status "Rejected." Is anything actually rejected today, or are you working from memory?
  • Open your Facebook page and look at the Boost button. Clickable means the page is not restricted. Greyed out means it is; click "See details."
  • Business Settings, then Security Center. Does it say your business is verified?
  • Business Settings, then Business Info. What is your ad account creation limit, and do you already have a spare ad account created?
Where that sends you
  • All clear: Door 01
  • A rejected ad listed: Door 03
  • A restriction listed anywhere: Door 04
  • Not verified, or no spare ad account: Door 05
  • Never run engagement ads: Door 02, that is where the protection comes from
