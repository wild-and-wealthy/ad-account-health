01

What "healthy" actually means

Meta does not publish how it scores an advertiser. But the working conclusion among media buyers, and the one Keala runs his accounts on, is that a big part of your score is a ratio: ads approved versus ads rejected, over roughly the last 30 days. (Keala)

That one idea explains most of what follows. Every ad is reviewed on its own. The same video can pass in one ad set and fail in another, and that is normal, not a sign that something is broken. Health comes from the volume of clean, approved ads behind you, not from winning any single decision. (Marina)

Rejections cost you twice. Once in the ratio, and again in your CPMs. Keala's words: "your CPMs go up, but not for the right reasons. They're penalising you as an advertiser." A rising CPM after a run of rejections is a penalty, not a sign your audience got more expensive. (Keala)

One thing that does not count: organic posts. Your reels and stories do not touch ad review. Only ads count toward the ratio. Post organically because it is good for your audience, not because you think it is protecting the ad account.

02

The weekly check

Five minutes, same day every week. Put it next to whatever ritual you already have.

  • Open Account Quality first. Any restriction on any layer? Any ads listed as rejected? (Marina)
  • In Ads Manager, filter by "Rejected." Read what is live today, not what you remember. In July 2026 our eight rejections cleared silently by mid-August and we kept calling them blocked for weeks. (our account)
  • Is your engagement campaign still running, in its own campaign, separate from the funnel? If not, Door 02.
  • Business Settings, then Security Center: still verified? (Marina)
  • Do you still have a spare ad account created and sitting idle? (Jen)
03

What not to touch

Most self-inflicted rejections come from editing something that was already approved. Meta treats a meaningful change to a live campaign as a reason to review it again, and in a strict season that re-review can go against you even when nothing in the creative changed.

SafeSends you back through review
New ads in a new campaign.Changing budget or targeting on a live campaign. One member changed only a budget and lost ads over it. (Keala)
Adding an ad inside your existing engagement ad set.Switching an old campaign back on. It gets re-reviewed on today's rules, not the rules it was approved under. (Keala)
Archiving or deleting an ad.Pushing new ads through an old campaign that is still running. (Keala)
Leaving your winners completely alone.Restructuring a campaign after launch. Marina did this and her already-approved ads went back into review. (Marina)
Pausing does not take an ad out of review

Only archiving or deleting removes an ad from the review queue. Pausing leaves it there. Archive if you want to keep the data (it is reversible). Delete if you are sure. (Marina)

04

Delete, or toggle off?

You will hear both rules from us, and they are not in conflict. They are for two different situations.

Rejected creative gets deleted. Keala, Marina, and Jen all land in the same place on this. A rejected ad sitting in your account is a strike that keeps showing in Account Quality. Remove it.

Losing ads get toggled off, never deleted. An ad that ran clean and just did not perform is part of the account's learning history. Turning it off keeps that history. Deleting it throws it away.

Two verbs, two situations

Rejected: delete. Underperforming: toggle off. If you remember nothing else from this page, remember that.

05

Words and images

Two policies catch almost everyone in our world: Personal Attributes and Financial Claims. (Marina)

Personal Attributes means Meta does not allow an ad to assert or imply things about the person watching it: her finances, her body, her health, her situation. The fix is to describe rather than accuse. "To the mama who feels behind no matter how much she gets done" describes. "Are you a mom who feels behind all the time?" accuses. Same person, opposite outcome. And never state an age. Use life-stage language instead.

Financial Claims is the one that has actually bitten us. Our account's entire rejection history sits under one policy, and the trigger is language about how money or compensation works. The full list of phrases is in the flagged-words box on Door 03. Bookmark it.

The income ladder, from Keala: safest is not to mention income at all. Safer is third-party framing. Riskiest is direct second-person how-to. And Meta judges mostly what is seen right after the click, so a line deep inside the webclass is far lower risk than the same line on the landing page. (Keala)

For statics, the short version is "no claims in images, no flags." For engagement content, the safe lanes are parenting, wellness, self-care, time, overwhelm, mental load, identity, courage. That content gets waved through. (Keala, Marina)

06

Crackdown season protocol

Meta moves in seasons. When the strict one arrives, the whole group feels it at once: special-category flags, ads killed after four days live, new campaigns that will not clear. This is what Keala had everyone do in August 2026. (Keala)

  1. Verify the business. Two minutes with your EIN and website. Business Settings, then Security Center.
  2. Delete the rejected ads out of the account. Do not leave them sitting there.
  3. Run engagement ads today using posts you already have that have nothing to do with the business, while you film new value videos. Door 02 has the settings.
  4. Leave your old, still-running campaigns alone. Ads that were live before a crackdown usually stay fine. Do not switch an old one back on, and do not push new ads through it.
  5. Do not launch new direct-response campaigns this season if you have something working. Run vanilla, get your ratio up, and wait it out.
  6. Warm a backup ad account now. Start engagement ads on your spare so it has a clean history before you ever need it.

Keala has lost two fan pages over the years, one with about ten million dollars of ad spend behind it, for no reason Meta ever explained. Spares are not the fallback. Spares are the plan.
